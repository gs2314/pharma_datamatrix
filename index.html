    How the scan is captured

    Keystrokes are intercepted at keydown level while the scan box is focused, so the invisible FNC1 bytes (emitted by the scanner as Ctrl+] or as an Alt+numpad 029 sequence) land in our buffer as real separators. The payload is then parsed into its GS1 Application Identifiers — 01 GTIN, 17 expiry, 10 batch, 21 serial — validated against the GS1 General Specifications (§3.2 for lengths, §7.11 for the AI-82 character set, GTIN Mod-10 check digit, YYMMDD with the DD=00 last-of-month convention), and stored as structured data.

    Getting the payload back into the validator

    Three interchangeable paths per row, all driven from the same stored structure:

    1. Scan — opens a modal with a fresh, standards- compliant GS1 DataMatrix rendered live from the parsed AIs via bwip-js (BWIPP, bcid: "gs1datamatrix", bracketed-AI input). Point your hardware scanner at the screen; the validator receives the exact same keystroke sequence it would receive from the original printed box, FNC1 included. This path does not rely on any assumption about how the gov portal handles clipboard paste.
    2. Copy — canonical pure-digit payload in order 01 · 17 · 10 · 21, no control characters. Works with portals whose edit control strips control characters on paste (most web portals, including HMNO / EMVS).
    3. Raw — the original scan with FNC1 intact. For strict-GS1 receivers (ERP / warehouse systems).

    Focus the gov validator's scan field on another machine or window, then point your hardware scanner at this DataMatrix. The scanner sends identical keystrokes to what the original box would have — FNC1 included — so the validator treats it as a live scan.
